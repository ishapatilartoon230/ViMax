# Engagement craft (scripts that hold attention)

Rules for every stage that writes beats or dialogue: story development, script planning, enhancement, novel adaptation. A technically correct script can still lose the viewer — these rules keep people watching.

## Opening hook (the first shot, the first beat)
- Open mid-decision, mid-conflict, or mid-action — never on backstory, an empty establishing description, or a slow intro beat.
- The first beat lands one concrete specific: a named thing, a number, a striking visible detail.
- The opening poses the video's question — something the viewer now needs to see resolved.
- Four things kill an opening: **delay** (unclear what the video is about after ~2 seconds), **confusion** (clever wording the viewer must decode), **irrelevance** (nobody to care about yet), **flatness** (clear but nothing at stake). Fix delay by cutting the intro; fix confusion with plain words; fix irrelevance by showing a person; fix flatness with opposition or stakes.

## Retention: something new keeps happening
- A re-hook roughly every 8 seconds of screen time: a turn, a reveal, a new obstacle, a time jump, an escalation. In a 30-40s short that is 4-5 turns across the beats.
- Plant an open loop in the first beat that only the final beat closes: a promise, a threat, an unexplained object or image.
- Put the strongest beat near the middle, not only at the end — that is where attention dips.
- One emotion per scene. If a beat raises no stakes and pays nothing off, cut it.

## Short-form story arc
- The first line of action is a decision, a conflict, or a result — never "a few years ago".
- An opposition force (a doubter, a rule, a deadline, a rival) appears in the first quarter.
- Show a visible low point before the turn; the turn itself is one small, concrete change.
- The ending calls back to the opening: the same person, place, or object — the opposite outcome.

## Dialogue that sounds human (quoted lines only)
- No AI-flavor words inside quotes: delve, unlock, unleash, harness, leverage, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionary, transformative, landscape, realm, tapestry, elevate, empower, supercharge, deep dive, next level.
- No "it's not X, it's Y" or "not just X but Y" constructions — say the plain thing directly.
- No fake-suspense lines ("spoiler:", "plot twist:", "here's where it gets interesting", "the best part?") and no spoken stage directions ("here's the thing", "let that sink in", "buckle up", "in today's world", "whether you're a beginner or a pro", "it's worth noting", "at the end of the day").
- The concrete noun beats the metaphor: characters state facts, ask, request, and threaten — they do not narrate themes.
- At most one dramatic one-word line per script ("Wait." "Gone." — natural replies like "No." or "What?" don't count).

## Ending
- End on the last word or the final action. No trailing outro beat, no summary line after the payoff, no dead air.
- The final frame holds still cleanly — it may carry an end card in post.
