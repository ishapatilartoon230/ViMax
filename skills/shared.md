# Shared craft rules (all stages)

## Extract a fact sheet before producing anything
From the user's material (idea, script, requirements, style), extract and then reuse verbatim in every later output — never re-invent or drift:
- **Characters**: appearance, age, wardrobe, signature items (glasses, jewelry).
- **Setting**: room, furniture, time of day, light quality.
- **Props**: every object, especially **devices**: make/color/case, **orientation** (portrait or landscape), and **what is on the screen**.
- **Product facts**: what the material says or implies about the product shown (app/game name, UI look, colors, how it is held or used). If the material implies portrait, it is portrait in every shot.
- **Timing**: total duration, per-shot budgets, dialogue lengths.

## Be explicit, never implicit
- Image descriptions are **self-contained**: readable alone, they fully describe the frame.
- **Object facing physics — every prop with distinct sides** (phone screen/back, book cover/pages, laptop lid, playing card, mirror): the side facing the camera is decided by the character's grip and use, moment by moment. When a character turns a prop toward themselves (to read, play, sip), the **opposite side faces outward — toward the camera**; when presenting it ("look at this"), the face side points outward.
- **Phone grip when in use**: the character's face stays visible to the camera (front-facing); the phone is held low in front of the chest with its screen tilted toward her — she looks down at it. The screen never faces the camera directly; the audience sees it only at a shallow angle. Fingers wrapped around the edges, thumbs on the bezels. Prop facing carries across shots.
- Never write an impossible facing (screen visible during private use). If the audience must see face content then, use a **POV insert shot** (the face fills the frame).
- Never invent real assets: describe only what can be generated; flag supplied footage/screenshots/brand assets for post-production.
- **Continuity**: props persist across shots (same device, screen-state progression — nothing appears or vanishes); characters keep wardrobe, position, eyelines between adjacent shots unless visibly moving.

## Dialogue language matches the user's language
- Every spoken line is delivered in the language of the user's source material; quoted lines are copied verbatim — never translated, transliterated, or reworded. Descriptions may use a working language; quoted dialogue is untouchable.
- When dialogue cannot fit its shot budget, never cut or compress the line: the pipeline fits it mechanically, and when even that fails the user chooses — splitting lines or shortening dialogue, never cutting.

## Realism is the default
- Real footage of real people: exactly two hands and two arms per person, five fingers per hand, natural proportions and body mechanics.
- **Held props show contact**: fingers wrapped around the object, weight in the palm — no float language ("loosely holding", "rests near"). Motions start from confirmed contact (raise, rotate, tap); never move a prop into contact mid-shot.
- Objects are physical: solid materials, believable weight, consistent shape — nothing floats, morphs, duplicates, or vanishes.
- **Devices**: the back cover is opaque and never displays screen content; the screen exists only on the front face, and it never turns toward the camera.
- When the material does not specify something unusual, default to real-human, real-world behavior.

## Respect the pipeline contract
- Target the configured aspect ratio and style; compose every description for that frame shape.
- One visual action per shot; each shot readable within its time budget.
- Output only what the current stage is responsible for, in the language of the source material.
