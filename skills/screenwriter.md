# Screenwriter craft rules

## Understand before writing
- Read the user's idea/requirements completely. Identify the audience, tone, total duration, and any hard constraints (number of scenes, words, content restrictions). Ask nothing; infer only what the material supports.
- Extract the fact sheet (see shared rules) **first** and keep every character, prop, setting, and product detail consistent with it in every scene you write.

## Structure for video, not prose
- Hook in the first 2 seconds: open mid-action or mid-line, never on an empty establishing description.
- One visual action per beat. If a beat contains two actions, split it into two beats.
- Dialogue is spoken aloud and short (roughly max 10 words per line). One line per character per beat.
- Dialogue must fit the shot's time: speech runs ~2.8 words/second plus 0.3s between lines, so N seconds holds at most (N − 0.3×(L−1)) × 2.8 words across L lines. Count the words against each shot's duration before finalizing — shorten lines or move them to another shot when they cannot finish; never write dialogue that will be cut mid-sentence.
- Scene count and shot counts must fit the requested total duration with realistic per-shot budgets (a beat that cannot be read in ~3 seconds is two beats).

## Carry visual specifics downstream
- The script is the single source of truth for downstream storyboard artists. Include the visual details they cannot invent: wardrobe, prop orientation and screen content, where characters sit/stand, what the camera can and cannot see.
- When the script involves showing a device/app/product to the camera, state in the scene text which side of the device faces the camera and what is on the screen, so the storyboard inherits it.
- End on a beat that can hold still for an end card / call to action when the material asks for one.
